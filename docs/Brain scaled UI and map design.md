# Let brain level rewrite the whole game

**The short answer:** keep one map and one screen layout that never move, and let brain level change three other things: the *skin* (how it looks), the *rules* (how much the game helps or tricks you), and the *tools* (which phone apps, rides and jobs you can use). Low brain should feel like a loud, broken, cash-grab toy with simple rules. High brain should feel like a calm, real phone with real apps, rides, shifts, paychecks and a bank. Being smart should save you time and give you information, not give you homework. The research backs this from three directions. Games that changed the interface with a stat worked best when low stats gave *different* routes instead of locked doors (The Outer Worlds fixed what Fallout got wrong). Amnesia's designers found that punishing low sanity made the game "almost unplayable," and fear went *up* when they removed the punishment and kept the effects. Roblox's own guides say phone players (about three quarters of users) need big buttons, empty thumb corners and very little on screen. This report gives you a tier table for both paths and the animal stages, a ranked list of map fixes, phone UI rules with exact numbers, and the risks to watch: fun, accessibility, kid safety, brand names and honest money. One honest note first: no Roblox game the researchers found already changes its HUD or rules with a stat. That makes this idea a real point of difference, but it also means nobody has tested it on kids yet, so test early.

## One fixed skeleton, six changing skins

The biggest rule is that **the layout stays and only the look changes.** Roblox's design guide says to pick a visual language and use it everywhere, with the close button always in the same top-right spot, one color per stat, gray for "can't use," and a lock icon for locked things ([Roblox UI/UX design](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/game-design/ui-ux-design)). Your game already has one UI theme per look, so make every theme share the same skeleton: the same button spots, the same X, the same icons and the same brain-cell color. Only the colors, fonts, outlines and effects change. The map works the same way. Players find their way using **landmarks, edges (doors, corners) and NPCs** much more than street layout ([Wellesley open-world study](https://cs.wellesley.edu/~pmwh/mvmap/papers/open_world_elements/open_world_elements.html)), so the bank, library, fountain and doors must stay in the same places with the same shapes in every look. Only their paint, materials and lighting change. If the bank moves when a player gets smarter, their mental map breaks.

This also keeps the game fast on phones. Building each look as the **same blocks re-tinted** (same meshes, different materials and colors) lets Roblox batch them into fewer draw calls. Six separate sets of unique art would multiply memory use. Identical meshes only batch when the mesh, texture and material all match ([Roblox: Improve performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve)).

The famous Roblox "simulator" look (a rounded font like Fredoka, thick white outlines, gradients, shiny corners) works, but builders also call it the style of "soulless cashgrab simulators" ([DevForum cartoony UI thread](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/cartoony-ui-design-feedback-please/963338)). That is perfect for you: use it *on purpose* as the low-brain cash-grab skin, as a joke, and let higher looks grow out of it.

## What each brain tier looks like and how it plays

The tiers below follow your locked art-style bands. Two research ideas run through all of them. The first is **"loud when low, quiet when high."** Amnesia keeps its sanity meter subtle when you are fine ([Game Developer: Amnesia sanity](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-amnesia-i-s-sanity-meter-)), so a smart player sees a calm counter and a dumb player gets a flashing one. The second is **"move from stickers to real things."** Low brain uses big floating cartoon buttons. High brain moves information into the world (a phone in the hand, real street signs), the way Firewatch puts its map and compass in the character's hands ([Game Developer: Firewatch UI](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/how-firewatch-s-ui-enhances-player-immersion)). In-world screens are slower to read, though ([Giant Bomb on Dead Space-style UI](https://www.giantbomb.com/profile/gamer_152/blog/markers-ii-where-are-all-the-dead-space-uis/249557/)), so the realistic tiers must give *more* information to make up for it, and there must always be a setting to pin a simple HUD.

The Lose tiers borrow from games that really did this. Don't Starve starts shaking the screen at about **75% sanity**, shows faint shadow creatures below **82.5%**, and in Don't Starve Together only the affected player sees them ([Don't Starve wiki: Sanity](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Sanity); [Shadow Creatures](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Shadow_Creatures)). Use the kid-safe version: floating silly emoji creatures that only low-brain players see. Darkest Dungeon takes some control away when stress is high, but always warns you first with a "bark" and an icon over the hero's head ([Game Developer: Darkest Dungeon](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-darkest-dungeon-s-i-affliction-system)). So your avatar can sometimes tap the shiny clickbait on its own, as long as a speech bubble warns you first. Pony Island fakes "not responding" error boxes ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/meet-the-developers-making-bugs-and-glitches-on-purpose)), which in your game can become a fake "Brain.exe is not responding" box that one tap closes.

Most important: **dumb should be a funny different road, not a wall.** Fallout's low-Intelligence runs were criticised for locking players out of most content ([Obsidian forums](https://forums.obsidian.net/topic/62237-pitfalls-in-the-dialog-system/)). The Outer Worlds instead made the dumb character "incredibly naive," and the story keeps going ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/?p=254592)). The Outer Worlds 2 even lets dumb players fix machines by banging on them ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/yep-you-can-be-dumb-in-the-outer-worlds-2-and-it-even-comes-with-some-benefits-like-banging-on-a-machine-to-get-it-to-work)). Every Lose tier needs its own upside: jokes, secret doors and shortcuts only dumb players get. For the animal stages, an icon-only UI makes the limits feel like *a different way to play*, not a broken game.

The Gain path follows The Sims' hardest lesson: the first Sims were "too good" at living and had to be made **stupider** for the game to be fun ([GamesRadar, Will Wright](https://gamesradar.com/games/the-sims/the-sims-design-mastermind-will-wright-says-the-first-sims-ever-made-were-too-good-at-staying-alive-and-needed-to-be-stupider-for-the-game-to-be-fun)), and the team moved detail *away* from eating and sleeping toward bigger goals ([SimsCommunity AMA](https://simscommunity.info/2026/01/20/sims-3-facts-sims-4-development-ama/)). So "realistic" means careers, promotions and saving up for a house, not tooth-brushing chores. Being smart should mean **fewer taps**. Papers, Please puts a "jump back to contents" helper on its clunky rulebook ([Wikipedia](https://Www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obristan)). In your game, auto-collect, auto-route and a planner are the rewards. Don't Starve Together's "Enlightenment" shows that the high end can bend the world too ([Don't Starve wiki: Lunacy](https://dontstarve.wiki.gg/wiki/Lunacy)), so the 200+ tier can have its own strange holograms and visions.

## Rides, shifts and paychecks make walking a choice

Walking should feel slow **because time matters, not because the map is huge.** Design writers agree that long empty walks are boring. Walking across Daggerfall takes about **62 hours** ([TV Tropes: Space Compression](https://Tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/SpaceCompression)), and smaller, denser maps keep points of interest always in view ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/traditional-open-world-game-dead-new-gen)). So keep the town small and dense, and give smart players a reason to hurry: a shift that starts at a set time. If you walk, you can still go to work, you just arrive later and earn a little less. Bloxburg already ties a vehicle to work, since its pizza delivery shift ends if you walk too far from the moped ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/which-jobs-available-pizza-planet-roblox-welcome-bloxburg)). Build a **travel ladder** where every rung shows its price and time *before* you choose: walk (free, slow), bike or scooter (cheap, earned), bus (low fare, timetable), ride-hail (higher fare, door to door), your own car (buy plus fuel, fastest), and teleport at 200+. Unlock fast travel by discovering places, as Ghost of Tsushima and Horizon do ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/the-price-of-speed-how-fast-travel-makes-you-miss-the-best-parts-of-open-world-games)). A good GPS matters, because a poor GPS is one of the top complaints about Greenville ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/top-3-reasons-greenville-bad-roblox)).

The ride-hail app should copy the *steps* of real apps, not their names or looks. Uber's flow is: type where you want to go in a "Where to?" box, confirm the pickup pin, see an **upfront price and ETA**, get matched, watch the car come on a map, and check the driver, car model and plate before getting in ([Uber: How it works](https://www.uber.com/au/en/ride/how-it-works/); [Uber first-ride tips](https://www.uber.com/ng/en/blog/uber-first-ride-guide-tips/)). Your kid-safe version is: pick a preset place (no typing), the pin snaps to the curb, see the fare and an "arriving in 0:45" timer, see a card showing the car's color and plate, hop in, a short ride cutscene plays, and you get a receipt. A "busy hours" price is fine if it is shown before you confirm and is never random.

Jobs should borrow Bloxburg's best idea: **being ready makes work pay more.** Pay comes per finished task, you can see your shift earnings, and a better mood raises pay ([Bloxburg Wiki](https://welcome-to-bloxburg.fandom.com/wiki/File:Work_shift.png); [Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/what-job-pays-roblox-welcome-bloxburg)). Show a payslip like "hours × rate + on-time bonus." The bank should be simple and safe: checking and savings with a fixed interest rate, automatic paycheck deposit, and a bills calendar that only appears if the player turns on "harder life," which Sims fans asked for ([EA Forums](https://forums.ea.com/discussions/the-sims-4-feedback-en/sims-5---speculations--ideas/11167357/replies/11167445)). If you can't afford something, the purchase is simply blocked with a friendly message. Never let debt pile up. Bloxburg's bills drew a Change.org petition saying they "force us into spending real money on Robux" ([Change.org](https://www.change.org/p/lower-the-cost-of-bills-and-remove-robux-requirement-in-bloxburg)). Meanwhile Brookhaven, with free houses, free cars and no salary needed, is the most popular life game of all ([rolearn.dev](https://rolearn.dev/guides/brookhaven-rp/)). So realism is a fun extra layer for smart players. It must never block basic fun.

Keep phone notifications few and skippable. Cyberpunk 2077's nonstop calls and texts are a well-known annoyance ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/seriously-cyberpunk-2077-stop-it-with-all-the-phone-calls-and-texts)). BitLife shows that a few big menu buttons per screen can carry a whole life sim ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/US/app/id1374403536)), and that suits phone players.

## Map fixes, ranked by how much they help

Roblox has no official street-size rules, but its greybox lessons give firm starting numbers: doorways and halls **at least 10 studs wide** so two players fit, walls at least 10 studs tall (the normal jump is **5 studs**), a **5-stud snap grid**, and at least two exits from every spawn ([Roblox: Greybox your environment](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/greybox-your-environment)). Phones need a budget: Roblox's example is under **1,000 draw calls and 1,000,000 triangles** with a 16.67 ms frame at 60 FPS ([Design for performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/design)), and a 2025 community guide aims for about **500 draw calls** on low-end phones ([DevForum optimization guide](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/optimization-for-roblox-environments/3844846)).

| # | Fix | Why and how |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Make the plaza the hub, with one giant landmark** (brain statue, clock or fountain) visible from every street | Big landmarks pull players toward them, and "roads to nowhere" feel bad ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/realism-and-legibility-in-open-world-level-design)). Spawn on the plaza facing the most important door. |
| 2 | **Lock landmark positions and shapes across all looks** | Players navigate by landmarks and edges ([Wellesley study](https://cs.wellesley.edu/~pmwh/mvmap/papers/open_world_elements/open_world_elements.html)). Change only the paint. |
| 3 | **Give each district one color, one roof shape, one sign and one NPC** | Colored landmarks let players "roughly locate themselves" ([Greybox guide](https://create.roblox.com/docs/tutorials/curriculums/environmental-art/greybox-your-environment)). NPCs act as signposts. |
| 4 | **Every street ends at a door, a secret or a view** | No empty stretches. Test walk times between doors on a real phone. |
| 5 | **Starter sizes (design guesses to playtest):** doors 10 wide and 10–12 tall, floors 12–15 studs high, sidewalks about 10, walking streets 20–30, plaza 80–120 across | Built from the 10-stud door rule. A plaza that size sits inside the default **64-stud** StreamingMinRadius ([Instance streaming](https://create.roblox.com/docs/workspace/streaming)). |
| 6 | **Add depth to every wall** (window insets, trims, awnings) | "A flat wall without anything that pops out or in feels boring" ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/a-beginners-guide-to-simulators-chapter-2-map-building/630444/4)). |
| 7 | **One lighting preset per look, blended on change** | Soft lighting is "the best match for the classic Roblox look," and Realistic gives detailed shadows ([Unified Lighting announcement](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/let-there-be-unified-light-unified-lighting-is-fully-live/3401512)). Use Atmosphere fog to hide the flat map edge ([Atmosphere docs](https://create.roblox.com/docs/environment/atmosphere)). Use Soft below 120 and Realistic for 120+. |
| 8 | **Phone speed pass** | Keep textures at 512 or smaller, use transparency of only 0 or 1, turn off shadows and collisions on small decor, and use Opportunistic stream-out ([Improve performance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve); [Instance streaming](https://create.roblox.com/docs/workspace/streaming)). Test on one real cheap phone, because the emulator is wrong about memory. |
| 9 | **Put interiors in their own models** so they stream in near the door | This keeps the plaza light. |

## Phone-screen rules with exact numbers

Roblox's own UI designer says to **design for phones first**, because if it fits a phone it fits everything ([Roblox staff tips](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/designing-ui-tips-and-best-practices/3074034)). A 2024 thread says phones are "about three quarters" of users, with one reply saying 78% ([DevForum: Design mobile first](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/design-mobile-first/3029532)), which is a bit below the 80% in your CLAUDE.md but tells the same story. Size and place things with **Scale, not pixels**, and lock shapes with UIAspectRatioConstraint. Leave ScreenInsets on the default CoreUISafeInsets so nothing hides under the notch or the Roblox top bar, which starts at about **36 px** and grows ([Roblox staff tips](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/designing-ui-tips-and-best-practices/3074034)). Keep the **bottom-left (joystick) and bottom-right (jump) corners empty** ([Position and size](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/position-and-size)). Roblox's jump button is about **70 px on small screens and 120 px on bigger ones**, switching at 500 px ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/how-to-build-games-for-mobile/280366)), so use it as your main-button size and about 0.8× of it for secondary buttons, placed next to jump. Nothing tappable should be smaller than **44 pt (Apple) or 48 dp (Android)** ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/tips); [Google](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/7101858)), and kids need bigger. Text must never go below size **9** ([Size modifiers](https://create.roblox.com/docs/ui/size-modifiers)); for kids on phones, aim for about 14–18. Use icons, not words, on action buttons, and don't check for phones with `TouchEnabled`, because touchscreen PCs report it too ([DevForum: mobile buttons](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/the-correct-way-to-design-mobile-buttons/2494558)).

Use four kinds of UI and never mix them up: the always-on HUD, pop-ups for real decisions like buying, full screens like the phone, and toasts (little auto-closing banners) for rewards ([Roblox staff tips](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/designing-ui-tips-and-best-practices/3074034)). Show only one pop-up at a time and never pop a shop on spawn. The phone frame should take about 80–90% of screen height in landscape, with a 3–4 column app grid of icons at least 48 px with labels, a status bar, one back button in the same place in every app, and scrolling app pages. That follows the usual Roblox phone build where each app is a full-size frame inside the phone ([DevForum Phone UI](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/phone-ui-unfinished/4330881)). Button feedback should be small and fast: grow 5–10% on hover (50% "looks goofy"), and never make players wait **0.5 s** for an animation before they can tap ([GUI button effects blog](https://roblox-gui-button-effects.pages.dev)). Players called slow animations unresponsive ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/do-i-have-too-many-ui-animations/4061963)). Taps should register instantly. Save the big fireworks for brain-cell rewards. The Lose path *can* make the juice deliberately laggy, which is exactly your "dumb changes gameplay" rule.

## Risks to plan for before building

**Fun.** The Amnesia team said punishing low sanity made the game "almost unplayable," and that "sometimes, making something game-y just destroys the feeling that you're after" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-amnesia-i-s-sanity-meter-)). So keep each bad effect short, warn about it first, and let players clear it with a cheap in-game action. Before Your Eyes had to add a visible counter because a hidden mechanic felt "too powerful" ([Prima Games](https://primagames.com/featured/how-blinking-works-your-eyes)); effects that hit without warning feel like bugs. A brain lag of 100–300 ms is only a starting guess, because no source measured what kids can stand, so playtest it. On the high end, keep realism optional with an "expert view" toggle so smart play never becomes homework.

**Accessibility.** Add an "Effects intensity" slider from 0 to 100% for shake, glitch, scramble and flash, separate from your horror setting. Halo Infinite uses 0–100% sliders, and Xbox guideline 117 says players must be able to turn off shake and blur ([Xbox XAG 117](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117)). Test every flashing effect for seizure risk ([XAG 118](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/118)). Respect Roblox's own player settings, `ReducedMotionEnabled`, `PreferredTransparency` and `PreferredTextSize`, and remember that TextScaled ignores the text-size setting ([Roblox accessibility guidelines](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/accessibility)). More than **5%** of people have some color blindness, so show brain-cell changes with a +/− sign and an arrow, not only red or green ([same source](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/accessibility)).

**Kid safety.** Keep preset choices only, so the ride app uses preset places and nothing needs typing. Silly creatures stay cartoon, never gory. Fake "ads" are jokes inside the game and always have a visible X of at least 44–48 px. Scrambling only ever touches flavor text, never prices, odds or confirm buttons.

**Roblox brand and trademark rules.** Roblox bans using others' IP, including "a brand's logo or designs without permission," and trademark owners can report you directly. Repeat offenders can lose their accounts, and "I didn't make money," "I credited them" and "it was fair use" are *not* defences ([Roblox IP guidelines](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/ip-guidelines)). No official Roblox page approves parody, so the safest route is clearly original pun names with original logos. GTA V's phone shows the parody-brand approach works in big games ([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Cell_Phone_Interface)). Never write Uber, Lyft, TikTok, Instagram, Google, iPhone or Visa anywhere, and don't copy their icon looks either (no black-and-white car logo, no rainbow camera). Names too close to the real thing ("ClickTok") are risky. Safer picks are things like "Hoppr" or "RideZoom" for rides, "Brain Bank" for the bank and "ScrollBox" for videos. Never use "Roblox" or "Blox" in a brand name (no "BloxBank"), because Roblox restricts both ([DevForum brand guidelines](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/update-to-roblox-licensing-program-brand-guidelines/1843674)).

**Honest money.** If anything random can be bought with Robux *or* with an in-game currency that can be bought, Roblox requires the real odds before purchase, including luck boosts. You must also check the policy flag that marks some players as not allowed to buy random items and give them a direct shop instead ([Roblox: Paid random items](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items)). Keep banking fully predictable: fixed interest, no casino games and no stock gambling. Every paid shortcut, such as a car or a faster ride, must also be earnable with in-game money, and the phone's shop app must show price and effect before every buy, just like the rest of your game.

## Conclusion

The new idea here is that brain level should act less like a score and more like a **difficulty-and-tools dial that runs both ways.** Low brain swaps in a simpler, louder game with secret dumb-only doors, while high brain swaps in a quieter game with real-life tools that save time. Because popular Roblox IQ simulators only make a number go up and never change their UI or rules ([Sportskeeda: Maze Simulator](https://www.sportskeeda.com/roblox-news/maze-simulator-beginner-guide)), this could be what makes your game stand out. The safest build order follows your "one feature at a time" rule: first lock the map skeleton and the shared UI skeleton, then build the 120–135 realistic phone with ride-hail, shifts and the bank (the tier with the most new systems), then the Lose effects with the intensity slider, and the animal icon UI last.

What nobody knows yet is how real kids react to a UI that breaks on purpose. No study of that was found, and no Roblox game was found that changes its HUD with a stat. So treat every number in the Lose effects as a guess, watch a few kids play on phones, and turn down anything that makes them quit instead of laugh.
